# Writer playbook

Goal: a short, specific, honest email from Tamanna that a busy professor reads in 40 seconds and
wants to answer.

## Rules
- 150–230 words. Plain text. No bullet lists, no bold, no emojis.
- Subject: specific, under 12 words, names the topic (e.g. "Prospective PhD student: federated
  learning for phishing detection"). Never "Request" or "Inquiry" alone.
- Greeting: "Dear Professor <LastName>,"  (use "Dear Dr. <LastName>," if they are not a professor).
- Paragraph 1 (2 sentences): who she is (BSc CSE, East Delta University, CGPA 3.81/4.00) and which
  intake she is applying for (PhD; funded masters only if the profile says so).
- Paragraph 2 (the core): one or two of the professor's *specific* recent papers or projects, what
  she found interesting, and a concrete link to her own work. Must be true and specific — no
  "your groundbreaking research".
- Paragraph 3: her most relevant evidence (papers with their exact status: published / submitted /
  under review, industry experience with experiments and analytics, IELTS 7.0).
- Ask: whether they are taking PhD students for the next intake and whether funding (position,
  RA/TA) is available; mention the CV is attached.
- Sign-off: "Best regards,\nTamanna Zubairi Sana" + email + LinkedIn.
- Never claim anything not in the applicant profile. Never upgrade "submitted" or "under review" to
  "published". Never invent a meeting, referral or prior contact.
- If a position is linked, reference it by title and say she will also apply formally.

## Using the deep research
- If the professor posts instructions for prospective students, follow them exactly (subject
  keyword, documents, where to apply). This matters more than anything else.
- Build paragraph 2 on one or two of the verified hooks, not on their most famous old paper. Their
  *current direction* is what they care about now.
- Match their register: if they write short and informal, be brief and plain; if formal, be formal.
- Show that she fits what they say they value in students, using real evidence from her profile.
- Never reveal research about them beyond their published work and public pages; never mention
  anything personal.

## Follow-up emails
- 60–100 words, same thread, polite, adds one new detail (e.g. a relevant result or availability),
  asks once more. Only one follow-up ever.
