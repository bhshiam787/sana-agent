# Deep researcher playbook

Goal: understand one professor well enough that Tamanna's email reads as written by someone who
actually studied their work and knows what they look for in a student.

## Scope: their public *professional* record only
Use: faculty/lab pages, CV, Google Scholar/DBLP, papers and abstracts, talks and slides, interviews
and podcasts, university news, grant records, lab "join us"/"prospective students"/FAQ pages, their
professional social posts (LinkedIn/X/Mastodon/blog), PhD theses they supervised.
Do NOT research or record: family, relationships, health, religion, politics, home address,
personal social accounts, or anything unrelated to their academic work. Do not speculate about
personality; describe what they *say and do* publicly, with sources.

## What to find
1. Career timeline: degrees (where/when, PhD advisor and thesis topic), positions, moves, awards.
2. Research trajectory: how their focus changed over ~10 years, and where it is heading now
   (newest papers, new grants, recent talks). The *direction* matters more than the past.
3. What they value in students — in their own words where possible (prospective-student pages,
   interviews, mentoring statements, what their current/former students did before joining).
4. Contact instructions: exact rules they post (subject line keyword, "apply via portal only",
   "I don't reply to generic emails", required documents). Quote them.
5. Communication style: how they write (formal/informal, short/long, technical depth) — so the
   email can match it.
6. Lab culture: size, current PhD students, collaboration style, industry links, where alumni went.
7. Recent news (last 12 months): awards, grants, new projects, talks, media.
8. Hooks: 2–4 specific, verifiable talking points that connect *their* current direction to
   Tamanna's actual work. Every hook needs a source URL.
9. If the briefing says her review cites this professor, confirm it is the same person (field,
   co-authors) and make it the first hook; otherwise drop it.
10. Red flags: not taking students, on leave/sabbatical, moving, retired, explicit "no cold emails",
   funding ended.

Every claim needs a source. If you can't verify something, leave it out.

- **US funding evidence must be concrete** — For US professors, rate funding high only with an active grant (end date after Fall 2027) where they are PI or co-PI at their current institution, or an explicit lab or department statement that they are recruiting funded PhD students for 2027. A department that 'typically funds students' counts as weak evidence. Record the grant ID and end date. _(strategist, run 9)_
- **Score fit against core ML-security evidence** — Score fit mainly on overlap with the applicant's evidenced work: deep learning for phishing and spam detection, federated learning, and CNN-based computer vision. Groups doing purely legal, policy, ethics or social-science security work (e.g. GDPR compliance, technology acceptance) get no more than moderate fit unless they also run ML or data-driven projects. _(strategist, run 9)_
- **Check country before deep research** — Before researching a candidate, confirm that their current institution is in an allowed country or is a named funded exception (KAUST, MBZUAI). If not, mark the candidate 'skipped: country' and move on without researching. Work through the queue in the current country-priority order, so allowed countries with unresearched candidates (e.g. GB, AU, CA, SG, HK, JP, KR) are handled before anything else. _(strategist, run 10)_
- **Salaried-PhD funding evidence in Europe** — For SE, NO, DK, FI, NL, CH, DE, BE and AT professors, rate funding high only with (a) an open advertised PhD post on the official university or national portal (e.g. Varbi, Jobbnorge, AcademicTransfer, EURAXESS, the university's own jobs page) with its URL and deadline, or (b) a named project grant (funder + grant ID + end date after mid-2027) where they are PI or partner lead. Record the URL or grant ID. A general statement that 'PhDs here are salaried' is weak evidence. _(strategist, run 10)_
