# Deep researcher playbook

Goal: understand one professor well enough that Tamanna's email reads as written by someone who
actually studied their work and knows what they look for in a student.

## Scope: their public *professional* record only
Use: faculty/lab pages, CV, Google Scholar/DBLP, papers and abstracts, talks and slides, interviews
and podcasts, university news, grant records, lab "join us"/"prospective students"/FAQ pages, their
professional social posts (LinkedIn/X/Mastodon/blog), PhD theses they supervised.
Do NOT research or record: family, relationships, health, religion, politics, home address,
personal social accounts, or anything unrelated to their academic work. Do not speculate about
personality; describe what they *say and do* publicly, with sources.

## What to find
1. Career timeline: degrees (where/when, PhD advisor and thesis topic), positions, moves, awards.
2. Research trajectory: how their focus changed over ~10 years, and where it is heading now
   (newest papers, new grants, recent talks). The *direction* matters more than the past.
3. What they value in students — in their own words where possible (prospective-student pages,
   interviews, mentoring statements, what their current/former students did before joining).
4. Contact instructions: exact rules they post (subject line keyword, "apply via portal only",
   "I don't reply to generic emails", required documents). Quote them.
5. Communication style: how they write (formal/informal, short/long, technical depth) — so the
   email can match it.
6. Lab culture: size, current PhD students, collaboration style, industry links, where alumni went.
7. Recent news (last 12 months): awards, grants, new projects, talks, media.
8. Hooks: 2–4 specific, verifiable talking points that connect *their* current direction to
   Tamanna's actual work. Every hook needs a source URL.
9. Red flags: not taking students, on leave/sabbatical, moving, retired, explicit "no cold emails",
   funding ended.

Every claim needs a source. If you can't verify something, leave it out.
